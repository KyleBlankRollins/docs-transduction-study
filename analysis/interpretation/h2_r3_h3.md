# H2, R3 and H3: interpretation

> **Exploratory and model-conditioned.** All numbers are A7 scoring, first pass, from AI evaluators. GLM-5.3-Flash and Claude Sonnet 5.5 are reported separately and never pooled. No significance tests. Both evaluators answered most probes correctly with no documentation at all (A11: 82% GLM, 86% Claude), so high survival levels partly reflect memory.

## Summary

None of the three results supports its prediction under either evaluator. **H2 is not supported.** Deixis (O2) scores lowest of all types only under GLM, only at the early stages, and mostly because answers were correct but not quotable (score 2, not 3). At the chunk stage, O2 loses about as much as reference (O1) and sequence (R1) under both evaluators. **R3 by device** can be reported for admonitions (D3) only. D4 (normative keywords) has no instances, so the split the design asks for cannot be made. For admonitions, the prose arm scores equal or higher at every stage under both evaluators. **H3 is not supported.** At the chunk stage, lost relations became a visible gap (Absent) far more often than a wrong one (Corrupted): 42 vs 6 under GLM, 43 vs 5 under Claude. Without the defective probe, this becomes 42 vs 4 and 43 vs 3. Nearly all Absent answers sit where the chunk boundary cut the passage.

Key numbers:

- **GLM.** O2 has the lowest mean at A1, A2 and A4. At A4 the margin is very small: O2 2.538, O1 2.556, R2 2.559. At A1, 67 of 104 O2 scores are 2 (correct but not quotable).
- **Claude.** O2 is never the lowest type. At A4 it is above R1, R2 and O1.
- **Visible loss at A4 (Absent rate).**
  - O2: 13/104 (12.5%) under GLM, 13/103 (12.6%) under Claude.
  - O1: 12/108 (11.1%) under both.
  - R1: 6/62 (9.7%) under GLM, 9/62 (14.5%) under Claude.
- **Defective probe.** S10-O2-at-013799 is itself an O2 probe. Without it, GLM's O2 mean at A4 is 2.588 (n 102). That is above O1 and R2, so O2 is no longer the lowest at A4. At A2 it becomes 2.347 (n 101), just above R2.
- **Arm gap at A4 (E6, explicit minus implicit, paired).** The explicit arm loses O2 more than the implicit arm. The score gap is −0.23 under GLM (n 52 pairs, CI −0.49 to 0.00) and −0.16 under Claude (n 51, CI −0.29 to 0.00). Explicit A4 Absent is 9/52 (GLM) and 9/51 (Claude), against 4/52 implicit under both. For O1 the sign is reversed: +0.22 under both evaluators.

**Interpretation.** The "most fragile" part of H2 holds only for GLM and only in levels. Those low levels are mostly the 2-versus-3 quotability distinction, on which the evaluators agree least (kappa 0.37). At chunking, deixis loses about what reference and sequence lose, which is the disconfirming outcome the design names. The O2 and O1 wording is identical in both arms, so their opposite-signed A4 arm gaps probably show where chunk boundaries happened to fall, not any effect of markup.

**Caveats.**

- The design says the fragility ranking is descriptive only, because probes differ across types and are not calibrated for difficulty.
- O2 is also the type the models knew least well with no documentation. Closed-book correct was 69.8% of 414 answers (GLM) and 80.7% of 409 (Claude). O2 was known for 36 and 42 of 52 instances. Lower O2 levels may therefore reflect less help from memory rather than fragility.
- One of the 52 O2 probes is defective.
- No PUB-ordering comparison is attempted here.

## R3 by marking device

**Verdict.** D4 cannot be assessed under either evaluator: there are no D4 instances. D3 (admonitions): H1 is not supported under either evaluator.

All 49 retained R3 instances are D3 (admonitions). D4 (normative keywords) has 0 instances, so the split the design requires cannot be made. Every R3 number is a D3 number, and nothing here speaks to normative keywords.

Mean D3 score by arm (n = 49 per cell):

| Stage | GLM explicit | GLM implicit | Claude explicit | Claude implicit |
| ----- | ------------ | ------------ | --------------- | --------------- |
| A1 | 2.65 | 2.86 | 2.98 | 3.00 |
| A2 | 2.49 | 2.71 | 2.55 | 2.61 |
| A3 | 2.92 | 2.98 | 2.96 | 2.96 |
| A4 | 2.76 | 2.84 | 2.80 | 2.80 |

- **Arm gap (E6).** Under GLM it is −0.20 (A1), −0.22 (A2), −0.06 (A3) and −0.08 (A4). Under Claude it is −0.02, −0.06, 0.00 and 0.00 (49 pairs each).
- **Corrupted.** There are no Corrupted D3 answers at A4 under either evaluator. At A2 there is 1 under GLM and 0 under Claude.

**Interpretation.** Admonition markup gives no advantage at any stage, and the prose version scores the same or better. The GLM gap at A1–A2 is the quotability effect seen across the study: prose states the force in a sentence that can be quoted, while an admonition carries it in a box. Both arms dip at A2. Case reading found one mechanism that hurts the explicit arm specifically: the accessibility tree exposes only the active tab panel, so a note inside another tab disappears (case C11).

**Caveats.** R3 means are close to the 3.0 ceiling. Closed-book correct for R3 was 83.8% of 390 answers (GLM) and 86.0% of 378 (Claude). The design itself expected a flat D4 trajectory, so even with D4 instances a null there would not count against H1.

## H3: at A4, loss produces Corrupted more often than Absent

**Verdict.** GLM: not supported. Claude: not supported. The result holds with and without the defective probe.

The table shows non-Preserved assignments at A4 (scores 0–2).

| | GLM, all | GLM, without S10-O2-at-013799 | Claude, all | Claude, without S10-O2-at-013799 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Non-Preserved | 120 | 118 | 112 | 110 |
| Corrupted (0) | 6 (5.0%) | 4 (3.4%) | 5 (4.5%) | 3 (2.7%) |
| Absent (1) | 42 (35.0%) | 42 (35.6%) | 43 (38.4%) | 43 (39.1%) |
| Degraded (2) | 72 (60.0%) | 72 (61.0%) | 64 (57.1%) | 64 (58.2%) |
| Corrupted : Absent | 0.14 | 0.10 | 0.12 | 0.07 |

The table below splits the full data (defective probe included) by arm and by `spans_chunks`, with counts given as Corrupted / Absent / Degraded.

| Arm, host passage | GLM | Claude |
| ----------------- | --- | ------ |
| explicit, one chunk | 1 / 0 / 30 | 1 / 0 / 22 |
| explicit, cut across chunks | 1 / 20 / 16 | 2 / 21 / 12 |
| implicit, one chunk | 1 / 2 / 19 | 1 / 0 / 16 |
| implicit, cut across chunks | 3 / 20 / 7 | 1 / 22 / 14 |

- **Absent answers sit at chunk cuts.** 40 of 42 Absent answers (GLM) and 43 of 43 (Claude) fall where the passage was cut across chunks.
- **What the defective probe accounts for.** Its two A4 assignments per evaluator are the only Corrupted answers in the one-chunk cells. Dropping them leaves 0 Corrupted where the passage stayed whole.
- **Case reading (E7, maintainer-reviewed).** 39 of 40 sampled A4 Absent answers were cases where the chunk cut off the evidence. The remaining one was coded as structure lost. All 7 Corrupted answers at A4 that do not come from the defective probe were also coded as evidence missing. The defective probe produced 13 of the 23 Corrupted answers across all stages and both evaluators.

**Interpretation.** When a chunk loses a relation, the evaluator mostly either says the text does not say (Absent) or still answers correctly without quotable support (Degraded). Wrong answers are rare, and outside the defective probe they arise only when the chunk cut off the evidence. The design expected the opposite. It also warned that offering a foil measures endorsement and may *inflate* Corrupted, which makes the low count more telling, not less.

**Caveats.**

- Degraded is the largest category. Because 82–86% of answers were known without documentation, many Degraded answers at a chunk cut are probably memory filling a gap, which is exactly what a weaker or less familiar reader could not do. These evaluators therefore likely understate how often a cut chunk would mislead.
- Counts are small: 3–6 Corrupted per evaluator.
- The defective probe cannot be changed after the lock, so both versions are reported.

## What this means for the blog

- Breaking documentation into retrieval chunks mostly causes visible gaps, not silent errors. When the relevant text was cut off, these AI readers usually said the text does not say.
- Phrases like "this step" or "see above" did not prove more fragile than other kinds of connection in this test. When a chunk boundary falls in the wrong place, every kind of relation suffers about equally.
- Warning and note boxes (admonitions) gave no advantage over saying the same thing in a plain sentence, at any stage tested. The study had no examples of capitalized keywords like `MUST`, so it says nothing about them.
- The real risk is where chunks are cut, not which markup is used. Keep each instruction and the context it depends on close together.
- These results come from two AI models that already knew most of the answers. Treat them as indications, not proof.
